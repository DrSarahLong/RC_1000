Syllabus Scavenger Hunt

A five-minute check to make sure you know how this course actually works. Online, asynchronous classes go sideways fast when you're unsure where to turn things in or when they're due, so let's lock that in before Week 1 gets rolling.

How to play

Open the Syllabus and Schedule in new tabs and hunt for the answers. Pick one for each, then hit Check. Getting these right now saves you a world of confusion later.

Open the Syllabus →

1. Where do you submit assignments and check your grades?

AsULearn. Everything is turned in there, and every grade is posted there. The AsULearn button is on the menu bar at the top of every page.

2. When is your very first assignment, the First-Day Writing, due?

Tuesday by noon, on AsULearn. It's the one assignment all term that isn't due on a Sunday, it is not optional, and if Dr. Long doesn't receive it she'll assume you've dropped the class.

3. When is your weekly work due after that?

Sunday. Each week's module is due the Sunday of that week. Check in with the course several times a week, a crammed week shows.

4. How does this class meet?

Fully asynchronous. There are no class meetings. You work each module on your own schedule (by the Sunday deadline).

5. What's the required textbook?

Voices for Change, a rental e-book through AsULearn. Everything else you read is free and linked right in each module.

6. How is your grade mostly determined?

Your three essays and the digital portfolio (scored on rubrics), plus your Writer's Notebook (completion). The full breakdown is on the Syllabus.

7. Is the private writing in your Writer's Notebook shared with classmates?

No. It saves on your own computer and is never shared with classmates. You turn the whole notebook in twice (midterm and end of term) as a completion check.

8. How do you use Dr. Long's office hours?

Email first to set a time, then you meet on campus or in her Zoom Room. Her Zoom link is on the Syllabus.

9. What three things will you write this semester?

A personal essay, an argument grounded in your own life, and a public op-ed, which you'll gather into a digital portfolio built free on Google Sites.

Now you know how this works. Where to turn things in, when things are due, and how to reach me. Keep this site's menu handy, the Syllabus and Schedule answer almost anything, and I'm an email away. On to Week 1.

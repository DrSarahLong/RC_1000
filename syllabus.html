Course description

This section of RC 1000 emphasizes critical reading, thinking, and writing, taught through the craft of narrative nonfiction and the lens of expressive, therapeutic writing. You'll work with invention and discovery, analysis, synthesis, argument, and revision, and you'll write in three genres: a personal essay, an argument grounded in your own life, and a public op-ed, before gathering your best work into a digital portfolio you can add to throughout your career at App and share with future employers as proof of your communication and writing skills. We use various invention techniques first to help you find something worth saying, grounded in decades of research showing that writing is a way to think, feel, heal, and make meaning, well beyond transmitting information. The goal is for you to become a creative, confident writer who understands how writing works in the brain and on the body.

Our lens: expressive and therapeutic writing. Decades of research show that writing is a cognitive and emotional process that shapes how we think, feel, heal, and make meaning from experience. Grounding composition in narrative and reflective practice helps you discover writing as a method of inquiry and self-understanding, which in turn builds stronger critical thinking, a more authentic voice, and skills that transfer across academic, professional, and personal contexts.

Rhetoric & Composition mission statement

The Appalachian State University General Education Rhetoric and Composition program utilizes current pedagogical practices that encourage students and faculty to undertake the serious work of critical thinking and reflection; research and evidenced interpretation; and knowledge production as we engage and create texts across multiple genres, disciplines, modes, and contexts. When grounded in rhetorical ethics, the study of literacies, rhetorics, and writing can empower students to be responsible and reflective local and global citizens.

Course objectives

General education: read critically and think analytically; write effectively for varied purposes, audiences, and genres; build and support ideas with evidence; and reflect on your own writing process.

Course-specific: use invention and expressive writing to generate honest material; apply narrative-nonfiction craft (scene, voice, concrete detail) across genres; build and defend an argument with credible, cited sources; write for a real public audience; and present your work in a professional digital portfolio.

How this course works

This is a fully asynchronous, online course. There are no class meetings. Each week has one self-contained module that carries that week's teaching, practice, and writing, and you work through it on your own schedule.

Plan to check in several times a week rather than in one marathon; the writing compounds, and a crammed week shows. Everything for a given week is due the Sunday of that week (the final portfolio is due at the end of term). My office hours are by appointment, email me first to set a time, then we meet on campus or in my Zoom Room, and I answer email quickly, so reach out anytime.

Your Writer's Notebook (15%)

Your low-stakes writing, freewrites, invention, and reflection, lives in your Writer's Notebook, which saves privately on your own computer as you type. It's graded on completion, not quality: the point is that you do the thinking, because that's what sets you up to succeed on the projects. You turn the whole notebook in twice, once at midterm and once at the end of term, by downloading it and submitting to AsULearn (a PDF or a Google Doc, your choice).

Major projects

You'll write in three genres and gather your best work into a portfolio. The three essays build on one another, invention and craft carry forward from each to the next.

How feedback and grading work. For each essay the process is the same: you turn in a draft for my feedback (comments, no grade), you revise using that feedback, and then you turn in a final that I grade on the rubric. The feedback is where the real teaching happens; the final is where you show what you did with it. Your private notebook writing is separate and stays yours.

Teaching approach

This is a writing course first: learning to write well is the point, and the expressive method is the engine that helps you find something worth writing. I teach through worked examples and guided practice rather than lectures, and I design assignments that make you apply craft on the page, not read about it in the abstract. Much of what you'll write is personal, so the expressive and invention writing stays private, kept on your own device; you turn the whole notebook in twice (at midterm and end of term) for a completion grade, but it is never shared with your classmates, and you choose which pieces you develop into polished, graded projects. You set the pace within each week, you decide how far to go with any topic, and you can always choose a lower-stakes subject. Your growth as a writer is the goal, and I'll support anyone who takes that seriously.

Policies

The writing you turn in is your own
Your essays should be yours: your thoughts, feelings, and experiences all count as primary source material. Using outside, secondary sources is welcome and expected in Projects 2 and 3, and citations are necessary there, but all your work will be grounded in the personal, because that's where the passion lies.
Deadlines
Weekly module work is due the Sunday of that week. The notebook is collected twice (midterm and end of term). The final portfolio is due at the end of term.
Late work
Late project work is not accepted except under extraordinary, documented circumstances. Talk to me early, not after.
The privacy of your writing
Your expressive and invention writing lives privately on your own device as you draft. You turn the whole notebook in twice, at midterm and end of term, for a completion grade (I read it only as a check that you did the work), but it is never shared with your classmates. You never have to share anything you're not ready to.
AI / ChatGPT
Using AI is not plagiarism in this class, but I expect you to use it appropriately and ethically. See these guidelines for the ethical use of generative AI (Santa Clara University). Learning to use it well is part of the skill set, but this course is about developing your voice, so lean on it thoughtfully.

Read Your Own Draft Like an Editor

Paste your draft and this will x-ray it, coloring the moves that tend to help or hurt a personal essay. Then it walks you through the calls only you can make. It runs on your computer, and it never leaves it.

What this is, and isn't. This can't tell you whether your essay is good. No tool can; that takes a real reader (me, and your own trained eye). What it does is hold up a mirror: it marks the spots that are usually worth a second look, an announcing opener, a feeling told instead of shown, a tidy bow of an ending, so you can decide. The highlights are educated guesses, not verdicts. You're the judge. Nothing you paste is uploaded or saved anywhere but your own browser.

Throat-clearing

The warm-up sentences that announce an essay instead of starting it: "A meaningful experience I had was…," "Ever since I was little…," "This essay is about…." Almost always, your real first sentence is two or three lines down, where something actually happens. Find it and start there.

Showing vs. telling

"Telling" states a feeling or conclusion ("I was nervous," "it was amazing"). "Showing" gives the moment, the body, the detail, and lets the reader feel it ("my hands wouldn't hold the cup still"). You need some telling to move and reflect, but the moments that matter should be shown. When the tool marks a "telling" spot, ask: what's the scene underneath it?

The tidy bow

The neat life-lesson at the end ("I learned that everything happens for a reason," "it made me a stronger person"). It's the most common way a personal essay goes false. When you feel a bow forming, question it instead: is that lesson actually true? Always? The honest, unfinished thought is stronger than the tidy one.

Why "educated guesses"?

This tool matches patterns in your words; it can't understand your meaning. So it will sometimes mark a line that's actually fine, or miss one that isn't. That's expected. Treat every highlight as a nudge to look again, then trust your own judgment. Being able to overrule the tool is the skill you're building.

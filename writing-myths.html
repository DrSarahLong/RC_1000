Writing Myths

You've been writing for years, and picked up some beliefs about it along the way. A surprising number of them are wrong, and they're the exact beliefs that hold writers back. Let's clear them out.

Your call first. For each statement, decide: True, or Myth? Commit to an answer, then hit Check to see how you did and why. This is what the whole course runs on.

"Good writers get it right on the first try."

Myth. Nobody's first draft is their best draft, not published authors, not me. Good writing is rewritten. That's why we'll spend real time on and revision, and why a messy start is exactly right.

"You should know exactly what you want to say before you start writing."

Myth. You write to what you think, not to transcribe something you already finished in your head. Waiting until you "know" is why people stare at a blank page. Start messy; the thinking happens on the page.

"Being a good writer is a talent you're either born with or you're not."

Myth. Writing is a , like an instrument. It responds to practice and feedback, both of which this class is built on. Where you start is not where you'll finish.

"Revision means fixing your spelling and grammar."

Myth. That's , which come last. Revision means re-seeing the whole, its point, shape, and order. Confuse the two and you polish sentences you were about to cut.

"Writing is basically private self-expression, just getting your own feelings out."

Myth. Even a personal essay is , in a situation, to do something. Writing is a social and rhetorical act. Expressive writing helps you discover your material, but you then shape it for someone else. Both are true at once.

"Dumping your messy thoughts onto the page can actually help you think more clearly, and even feel better."

True, and there's real science behind it. Writing out worries or hard experiences and organizes your thinking (Ramirez & Beilock even found students who wrote about their worries before a test scored higher). We'll use this as an engine for invention all semester.

Here's what this class believes, and it's the opposite of the myths: writing is a learnable process of discovery, done for real readers, and the act of writing helps you think and feel better along the way. Everything we do this semester runs on that. Welcome.

Shitty first drafts

The writer Anne Lamott's famous idea (you'll read her this term): let the first draft be bad on purpose. You can't fix or improve a blank page, so the job of a first draft is just to exist. Getting it down and getting it good are two different jobs, don't try to do them at once.

Writing is discovery

Donald Murray and Peter Elbow taught that you write to find out what you think, the draft is the thinking, not a report of thinking you already did. That's why "just start" beats "plan it all first." The page is where your ideas actually take shape.

Writing is learnable

The "born writer" myth quietly gives people permission to give up. The truth researchers and teachers keep confirming: writing improves with deliberate practice and feedback, exactly what a writing course provides. Talent helps nobody who doesn't practice; practice helps everybody.

Revision vs. editing

Different jobs, done big to small. Revision re-sees ideas and structure (what's missing, repeated, out of order). Editing fixes sentences. Proofreading catches typos, last. We spend a whole week on real revision because it's where writing actually gets better.

Writing is social and rhetorical

One of the field's core ideas (Adler-Kassner & Wardle): writing always happens for an audience, in a context, to accomplish something. Even the most personal essay makes choices for a reader. So "express yourself" and "reach a reader" aren't opposites, good writing does both.

Writing to clear your head

Ramirez & Beilock (2011) found that students who spent a few minutes writing about their worries before a high-pressure test performed better, because offloading worry onto the page frees up working memory for the task. Getting the mess out of your head and onto paper is a real cognitive tool, not just a feeling.

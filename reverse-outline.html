Reverse-Outline X-Ray

The tool breaks your draft into paragraphs. You write what each one does. Laid out together, the skeleton shows you the gaps, the repeats, and the buried story, things you can't see while you're inside the prose.

This does no judging. It just splits your draft into paragraphs so you can label the job each one does (not what it's about, what job it does: "sets the scene," "explains the backstory," "the real turning point"). Private, saves in your browser only.

Now read it like an x-ray

Does it open on the heat?If paragraph 1's job is "announces the topic" or "gives background," your real opening is probably lower down. Move it up.
Do any two paragraphs do the same job?Two lines that say the same thing = repetition. Keep the stronger paragraph, cut or merge the other.
Is anything out of order?Does the skeleton read in an order that builds, or does a key moment land too early or too late?
Is the real story buried?Which paragraph's job is the true heart of the essay? Does it get enough room, or is it one line in the middle?

Major Projects

You'll write in three genres and gather your best work into a portfolio. Each project is scored on a handful of criteria, and the weekly modules walk you through building each one, step by step.

How to read a rubric. Each criterion is scored on one of four levels. Meets is the target: solid, full-credit work. Read the Meets column first, that's what you're aiming for on every line.
Exceeds goes beyond the target Meets the target (your goal) Developing partway there Not yet not present yet
Project 1 · Weeks 1–620%

Personal Essay

Narrative nonfiction · submitted as a Google Doc

A true story from your own life, found through expressive writing and shaped with real craft. You carry one meaningful experience all the way through invention, drafting, and revision.

The rubric

Scene & concrete detail
ExceedsVivid, precise scenes throughout; images do the work
MeetsOpens in a scene; key moments shown with sensory detail
DevelopingSome scene, a lot of summary
Not yetMostly abstract telling
Controlling idea (show and tell)
ExceedsA resonant insight that deepens; scene and reflection in partnership
MeetsA clear "so what," revealed through the story, not announced
DevelopingAn idea, but vague or stated flatly
Not yetEvents with no discernible point
Arc & earned ending
ExceedsReal tension builds; ending is earned and resists the tidy bow
MeetsHas an arc; the ending lands and feels earned
DevelopingFlat or list-like; ending too neat
Not yetNo arc; formulaic or missing ending
Craft & clean surface
ExceedsPolished, varied sentences; essentially clean
MeetsClear prose; few surface errors
DevelopingSome lapses in clarity or mechanics
Not yetFrequent errors distract
Project 2 · Weeks 7–1120%

Argument Grounded in the Personal

Research-based argument · MLA · submitted as a Google Doc

A claim that grows from something you've lived, then widened with credible outside sources. Same honesty as the personal essay, new job: taking a defensible position and backing it.

The rubric

Claim
ExceedsPrecise, debatable, well-qualified; genuinely at stake
MeetsA clear, debatable claim, sized to the evidence
DevelopingA position, but vague or overclaimed
Not yetA topic or fact, not an arguable claim
Evidence & sources (MLA)
ExceedsWell-chosen sources woven in; flawless MLA
MeetsCredible sources in quote sandwiches; consistent MLA
DevelopingSome evidence; dumped quotes or shaky citation
Not yetLittle evidence, or uncited
Counterargument
ExceedsAnticipates and answers the strongest objection, fairly
MeetsNames and answers a real counterargument
DevelopingMentions the other side but doesn't engage it
Not yetOne-sided; ignores objections
Voice & organization
ExceedsYour voice leads throughout; structure builds tightly
MeetsClear structure; voice survives the sources
DevelopingLoose order, or voice buried under quotes
Not yetDisorganized; no clear line
Project 3 · Weeks 12–1320%

Public Op-Ed

About 600–800 words · hyperlinked sources · submitted as a Google Doc

Your argument, compressed and aimed at a real reader on an editorial site your generation actually reads, someone busy and skeptical. Short, sharp, and timely, a piece you could pitch for publication.

The rubric

Hook & nut graf
ExceedsIrresistible hook; claim and "why now" land in seconds
MeetsA strong hook; clear nut graf with a news peg
DevelopingSlow open, or missing the news peg
Not yetNo hook; unclear what or why
Concision & public voice
ExceedsEvery word earns its place; unmistakably human voice
MeetsTight, plain, in a real public voice
DevelopingWordy or stiff in spots
Not yetPadded, hedged, or academic
Evidence (hyperlinked, fair)
ExceedsApt evidence, cleanly linked; scrupulously fair
MeetsClaims backed and hyperlinked; no cheap shots
DevelopingThin evidence, or missing links
Not yetUnsupported or misleading
Kicker & shape
ExceedsOne idea, perfectly shaped; kicker sticks
MeetsFocused; lands a real closing line
DevelopingWanders, or trails off at the end
Not yetShapeless; no ending
Project 4 · Weeks 14–1625%

Digital Portfolio

A published Google Site (free, no code) · submitted as a link

The culminating project: a small website that gathers your revised essays and presents you as a writer. It holds your three pieces, a bio, a philosophy of writing, and a reflective introduction on how you grew.

The rubric

Completeness
ExceedsAll pages present and thoughtfully arranged
MeetsHome, Bio, three essays, Philosophy, and Reflection all present
DevelopingA page or two missing or thin
Not yetMajor pieces missing
Revision
ExceedsStrongest piece markedly better than its draft
MeetsReal revision visible on the chosen piece
DevelopingLight edits only
Not yetDrafts uploaded unchanged
Reflection
ExceedsSpecific, honest metacognition; names growth and transfer
MeetsReflects specifically on how you grew as a writer
DevelopingGeneral or vague ("I learned a lot")
Not yetMissing, or purely a course review
Design, accessibility & link
ExceedsClean, accessible, polished; link works for everyone
MeetsReadable, alt text, real headings; public link opens
DevelopingSome design or accessibility gaps
Not yetHard to read, or the link doesn't open
Ongoing · completion15%

Writer's Notebook

Private, on-device · turned in twice

Your weekly module work, freewrites, invention, and reflection, lives here and saves on your own computer. It's graded on completion, not quality: full credit for doing the thinking. You turn the whole notebook in at midterm and again at the end of term.

Where and when each piece is due is on the Schedule. How the course runs and the full grading breakdown are in the Syllabus.
